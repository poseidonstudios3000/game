Art direction exploration — 5 directions

One ship. Five ways to draw the war.

Every board stages the same beat — player ship at the bottom, alien formation descending, a fight already in progress — so the directions compare honestly. Next to each playfield sits its style tile: the locked palette, enlarged sprite studies, and the rendering rules that keep the look consistent once we actually build the game.

Rendering rules

  • GRID LAW. Every mark snaps to one square cell — no anti-aliasing, no gradients. Mid-tones exist only as 50/50 dither checkers of two palette colors.
  • LIGHT. One source, high and left: a single solid highlight step, a single dithered shade step, then void. Nothing softer ships.
  • LINE. No outer black stroke — silhouettes cut hard against Void Navy, and interior color fields separate with 1-cell navy seams (canopy frames, engine gaps).
  • SHAPE. Chunky, symmetric, 45°/90° stair-steps only. Green/white is player tech, magenta is threat, orange-to-white is energy — readable at one glance from three feet.

Rendering rules

  1. Stroke, never fill. Every ship, alien, and HUD element is a closed luminous outline on the void; interiors stay empty so the field reads through everything — the one sanctioned fill is a filament-white hot core (rule 04).
  2. Light is the material. Forms are self-luminous — no external light, no cast shadows. Every stroke carries two glow passes: a wide soft halo under a tight hot line, like an overdriven vector beam.
  3. Hard geometric shape language. The player is a single forward wedge; enemies are down-pointing fangs and cellular hexes. Straight edges and pure primitives only — nothing organic.
  4. Color means faction. Ion cyan is always the player, magenta and violet are always hostile, and filament white is reserved for the hottest points on screen: cores, hits, and pickups.

SPRITE STUDIES

RENDER RULES

  • One line. Every sprite and HUD pill wears the same thick twilight-ink outline; sky layers go lineless so they fall back.
  • Two tones, hard edge. Flat fill plus exactly one darker shade per shape, cut on a straight or crescent edge. Light lives top-left. No gradients, ever.
  • Shape language. Pills, blobs and circles only — if a corner is not rounded, it does not ship. Enemies get pupils, antennae and tiny frowns.
  • Depth is shadow. Layers separate with soft oversized indigo drop shadows, never with line weight, texture or grit.
  • Color law. Bubblegum pink and mint teal are invader colors — Bloopers blush pink, Twinzos glow mint. The player flies cream, yellow and indigo. Munitions never share a hue: yellow bolts go up, pink orbs come down.

Rendering rules

  • 01NO OUTLINES. Forms read by value against the void; panel seams are gaps of Void Black, never strokes of light.
  • 02ONE KEY LIGHT. Everything is lit from screen-top: hulls grade Bone Steel into Gun Slate, alien flesh falls off into Oxblood.
  • 03SHAPE LANGUAGE. The gunship is chamfered and symmetric; everything alien is ribbed, curved, and deliberately asymmetric. Threat must read in silhouette at 16 px.
  • 04PLASMA IS EMISSION. #FF6A2B appears only where energy burns — engines, hostile munitions, alien eyes. Your guns run cold: tracers grade Bone Steel into white. If it is orange and inbound, it kills you. All other color is a tint or shade of the five swatches.

Rules of the craft table

  • Cut, never inked: no outlines anywhere — every colored shape sits on an offset torn-white paper core, and one hard drop shadow does all the separation.
  • One desk lamp, upper-left: shadows always fall down-and-right the same 2–3px, never blurred. Depth comes from stacking layers, not from shading inside a shape.
  • Blunt-scissors shapes: chunky convex polygons, no curve tighter than a thumb. Eyes, stripes, flames and star doodles are drawn on with crayon and chalk dashes — never cut.
  • Motion is physical: nothing glows or motion-blurs — paper wobbles, bobs, and flickers like it was nudged by a finger. The HUD stays handwritten on masking tape.
